## Where it comes from

On 5 October 2026, while cleaning up the branches of a project, an experienced developer discovers `git fetch --prune`. Not an obscure option: the answer to a question asked dozens of times without ever being put into words. "Why do I still see that branch that was deleted?"

This is not an isolated case. You can be senior and never have needed to understand remote-tracking references, because it never blocked long enough to stop and look. The resources exist, they are even excellent, but they are organised **by command**, for someone who already knows what to look for. Yet you always arrive with **a situation**.

This site was born that day.

## What it promises

- **Start from the situation**, not the command. A page's title is what you would type into a search engine.
- **Real outputs.** Every command shown was run, by a script versioned with the page, with the Git version indicated. Nothing is copied from memory.
- **Always the why.** A command without its mental model is forgotten within a week.
- **Git first, GitHub flagged.** What holds for GitLab or Bitbucket stays reusable.
- **Short.** A page reads in five minutes. What exceeds that becomes another page.
- **In French first**, for French-speaking developers of all levels, and in English since 7 October 2026. The French page remains the reference; the commands and their outputs are the same in both languages.

## Who

The project is started and maintained by [Hatimou Nabina](https://github.com/Hatimou-Nabina). It is open to contributions: a situation you lived through, a fix, a translation. The repository's `CONTRIBUTING.md` explains how, and the code of conduct what we expect from one another.
